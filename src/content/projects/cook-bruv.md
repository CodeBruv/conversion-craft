Cook Bruv started with a simple question: what happens when a business's ordering process is treated as a workflow instead of just a website?

The system connects the customer side of the business with the people responsible for running it.

A customer can browse available items, place an order, choose pickup or delivery, and make an online payment. The payment is connected to the order and verified before the order is treated as paid.

On the management side, staff can control the information customers see and manage incoming orders through defined stages. They can also manage menu information, availability, delivery rules, business information, customers, and operational activity.

The important part is the connection between these pieces.

Instead of:

Customer message → Staff checks details → Order is recorded manually → Payment is confirmed → Kitchen is informed → Customer asks for an update

the system provides:

Customer order → Payment verification → Structured order → Staff notification → Processing → Fulfilment → Status update

The restaurant is simply the first example of the larger product idea.

The same approach can be adapted to businesses such as laundry services, logistics, hospitality, and other service businesses where customers make requests and staff have to repeatedly coordinate information, payments, fulfilment, and status updates.

The principle is simple: understand how the business actually works, then turn that process into a practical digital system.
